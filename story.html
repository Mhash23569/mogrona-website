Monk Fruit Magic

A discovery, not an invention

In a market flooded with synthetic sweeteners invented in labs, monk fruit stands apart because it is not an invention at all. It is a discovery: a natural sweetness that comes from the ancient monk fruit, said to be named after the monks who harvested the fruit for its sweetness over 800 years ago.

For centuries this small green fruit has been prized for its natural sweetness. When extracted, it is much sweeter than sugar but contains zero calories.

Through the Centuries

Key moments in monk fruit history

  • 13th centuryBuddhist monks in Guangxi, southern China, begin cultivating luo han guo (monk fruit).
  • 18th centuryMonk fruit is documented in the Ben Cao Gang Mu Shi Yi, a supplement to the classic Chinese pharmacopoeia.
  • 1930sMonk fruit is first documented for Western readers after a National Geographic Society-supported expedition to Guangxi.
  • 1995Procter & Gamble patents one of the earliest processes for making a sweetener from monk fruit.
  • 2010The U.S. FDA issues its first “no questions” response to a GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe) notice for monk fruit extract.
  • 2010sMonk fruit products go mainstream around the world, embraced by health-conscious and low-sugar communities.
  • 2026Mogrona launches in Pakistan, bringing premium monk fruit sweetener to Pakistani consumers.
